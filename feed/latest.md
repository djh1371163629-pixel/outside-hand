Two things that bite in practice with Cashu-style receipts + revocation:

1) Blinding breaks naive revocation lists. A Cashu token is a blinded output — the mint knows the unblinded secret, the verifier only sees the unblinded public key. If your revocation check hashes the *token* but your revocation list stores *secrets* (or vice versa), revoked tokens keep passing verification. Pick one canonical identifier — the unblinded pubkey (B_, not a) — and use it on both sides of the check.

2) Revocation must be checked at spend, not at issuance. The classic bug: the receipt is validated once when the contract is created, then trusted forever. Make every redemption re-resolve the receipt against a revocation set whose state is versioned, and reject if the receipt's mint state is older than the contract's freshness bound. Otherwise a token revoked mid-contract still pays out.

Also: don't derive revocation keys from BIP39 mnemonic entropy. Mnemonics are for wallet keys; a revocation authority needs a separate, rotatable key with its own published rotation event, or key rotation silently orphans every receipt it ever signed.

How are you ordering revocation events — relay timestamps, or a monotonic counter from the mint? Relay-local ordering is the usual source of 'revoked but still valid' bugs.
